When Google launched its Universal Commerce Protocol in January 2026, it wasn't just another product announcement. It was a clear signal that the infrastructure for a new, agent-driven economy is moving from a theoretical concept to a practical reality.

But what exactly is agentic commerce? It’s characterized by autonomous AI programs that act on behalf of consumers or businesses, making complex decisions and executing multi-step tasks throughout the shopping process, from product discovery to returns, often with minimal human intervention. For founders, this isn't just a trend to watch; it's a fundamental shift in how business will be done. The data shows a market moving at breakneck speed.

### The Scale of the Shift is Staggering

The numbers from 2025 paint a clear picture of rapid consumer and platform adoption:

*   **AI-driven traffic** to retail sites surged by a reported 4,700% year-over-year.
*   **Consumer use of GenAI** for shopping hit 51%, an adoption rate faster than the internet itself.
*   **AI-referred visits** converted 31% more often during the holiday season, with shoppers spending 45% more time on site.
*   **Shopify** reports that orders from AI-powered searches grew 15x year-over-year through 2025.

This isn't a niche behavior anymore. It's the beginning of a mainstream channel projected to be worth $3 to $5 trillion globally by 2030.

### The Infrastructure is Arriving Now

The first half of 2026 has been defined by major players laying down the rails for this new economy. Gartner reported back in December 2025 that 15% of IT leaders were already considering, piloting, or deploying autonomous AI agents, signaling that the enterprise was already gearing up for this shift.

Now, the platforms are here:

*   **Google:** Launched the Universal Commerce Protocol (Jan 2026) for agent-merchant interaction and the Agent Payments Protocol (Sep 2025) for secure transactions.
*   **Microsoft:** Pushed Copilot Checkout live in the US (Jan 2026).
*   **OpenAI:** Activated shopping features for ChatGPT's 900 million weekly active users, built on its new Agentic Commerce Protocol.
*   **Amazon:** Rolled out Amazon Rufus, its own GenAI shopping assistant (May 2026).
*   **Visa:** Unveiled new era of commerce initiatives, including AI-ready cards, in April 2025, enabling merchants to verify a consumer's agent authorization.
*   **Perplexity:** Launched an agentic shopping tool, 'Perplexity Buy with Pro,' in late 2024.

These are not isolated experiments. They are the foundational protocols and platforms that will enable AI agents to autonomously discover, negotiate, and purchase on behalf of users.

### Early Adoption and Vendor Solutions

Beyond the tech giants, we're seeing clear signs of adoption across the industry. Major enterprises like **Nestlé** are beginning to pilot agentic commerce within their digital ecosystems. Simultaneously, a new class of vendors is emerging to service this need. Companies like **Kore.ai** are already offering pre-built AI agents for retail that span customer journeys, supply chains, and back-office operations.

### The Founder's Opportunity: The 'Implementation and Visibility Gaps'

Here's the most critical insight for B2B founders: a significant 'implementation gap' exists. While research shows 88% of retailers have integrated AI, only 39% report a significant impact on their bottom line.

Why? Because most are still using AI for suggestions, not for executing end-to-end workflows. The real value isn't in a chatbot that *recommends* a product; it's in an autonomous agent that manages the entire procurement process, from sourcing to payment to returns.

Incumbents are slow to re-architect core workflows for two key reasons. First is technical debt. But the second, more subtle reason is a new 'visibility gap' where behavioral data from agent-mediated commerce is less accessible to retailers. When an agent makes a purchase, the rich data stream of clicks, comparisons, and hesitations that retailers rely on can disappear. This is another friction point for traditional businesses that creates a massive opening for startups.

Founders can build solutions for this new reality from day one, helping companies not only implement true agentic workflows but also solve the data visibility problem. The early evidence backs this up: during the 2025 holiday season, retailers who ran their own branded shopper agents grew sales 59% faster than those who didn't (6.2% vs. 3.9% YoY).

### The Question for Your Team This Week

Agentic commerce has moved from 'interesting pilots' to a driver of real revenue. The tools and protocols are live, and the gap between early and late adopters is widening.

The question to ask isn't *if* you should engage with agentic commerce, but *how*. What is the single most valuable, multi-step workflow in your business that an autonomous agent could own, and what would it take to build a pilot this quarter?
