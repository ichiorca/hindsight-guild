# Agentic Commerce: What Founders Need to Know About Q2 2026

> The first half of 2026 brought major platform shifts from Google, OpenAI, and AWS. Here’s what the new landscape of AI-driven commerce means for B2B.


The starting gun for agentic commerce fired in January 2026. In a single month, Google launched its Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), Microsoft rolled out Copilot Checkout, and OpenAI, in partnership with Stripe, introduced its Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP).

These aren't just product updates; they are the foundational rails for a new kind of internet, where AI agents transact on behalf of users and businesses. For founders, this signals a fundamental shift in how products will be discovered, bought, and sold.

### Beyond Chatbots: What is Agentic Commerce?

Agentic AI moves beyond simply responding to commands. It enables autonomous agents to reason, plan, and execute complex tasks. In a commerce context, this means AI that can manage inventory, personalize product discovery conversationally, and even make purchases without direct human approval at every step.

While consumer-facing examples like Walmart's AI assistant 'Sparky'—which saw weekly active users jump over 100% in Q2 2026—grab headlines, the B2B implications are even more profound. One projection suggests that 90% of B2B buying could be intermediated by AI agents by 2028, driving over $15 trillion in spend.

### Key Advancements from the Last Six Months

*   **Platform Protocols Emerge:** Google's UCP and OpenAI's ACP are creating standards for how AI agents will interact with merchant catalogs. This is the new battleground for discoverability—think of it as SEO for machines, or 'Answer Engine Optimization' (AEO).

*   **Retail Giants Prove the Model:** In May 2026, Amazon launched the AWS Agentic Shopping Assistant for retailers. Walmart’s success with its own agent shows that AI-driven orders can be more valuable—orders from Sparky users were 35% larger on average.

*   **The Market is Scaling Fast:** The growth metrics from the past year are staggering. Generative AI traffic to retail sites saw a 4,700% year-over-year increase in July 2025, and Shopify reported that orders from AI-powered searches grew 15x through 2025. This momentum is reflected in the market valuation, which topped $1.1 billion in April 2026. Looking ahead, Gartner predicts that 40% of enterprise applications will feature task-specific AI agents by the end of this year, a huge leap from less than 5% a year prior.

*   **Financial Infrastructure is Adapting:** Recognizing the need for secure machine-to-machine transactions, major payment networks are building new rails. Mastercard's 'Agent Pay' and Visa's 'Intelligent Commerce' initiatives are designed to give AI agents a verifiable and secure way to transact on behalf of users, addressing critical hurdles in fraud and compliance.

### Navigating the Hurdles: What Founders Need to Watch Out For

While the opportunity is immense, the path to fully autonomous commerce is not without its challenges. The excitement around new protocols and platforms masks a more complex reality on the ground.

*   **The Trust Deficit:** A significant hurdle remains consumer and business trust. A majority of users are still uncomfortable with AI making final purchasing decisions or managing sensitive information without human oversight. This means the first wave of successful B2B agents will need to be transparent, reliable, and operate in high-trust, low-risk environments.

*   **Lack of Mature Standards:** While UCP and ACP are a major step forward, there is still a lack of clarity and mature, universal standards for how agentic AI will interface with the vast ecosystem of existing business systems. This fragmentation means founders need to bet carefully on which ecosystems to integrate with first.

*   **The Pace of Adoption:** Despite the rapid-fire announcements, the scaling of deeply-embedded agentic commerce will take time. Many potential customers and partners are, as some reports note, 'woefully unprepared' for this shift, with legacy data structures and a shortage of clear AI skills needed to manage these new systems.

### What B2B Founders Should Be Doing Now

The initial wave of adoption favors smaller, high-trust agents embedded into existing workflows, not complex, all-in-one solutions. This is good news for founders who can focus on specific, high-value problems.

However, as noted, many organizations are reportedly 'woefully unprepared' for this shift. The primary hurdles aren't just about AI; they're about data and infrastructure.

1.  **Audit Your Data Structure:** For your products to be discoverable and purchasable by an AI agent, your product data must be machine-readable and your delivery infrastructure API-ready. Is your tracking data normalized? Can an external system query your inventory and get a clean, structured response?

2.  **Rethink Your Payment Stack:** Most payment environments are designed for human interaction. The move to machine-to-machine commerce introduces new potential risks in fraud and compliance that need to be addressed proactively.

3.  **Focus on Back-End Operations:** The most immediate opportunities for agentic AI in B2B are in the back-end. Think supply chain autonomy, dynamic inventory management, and demand forecasting. These are areas where agents can deliver operational efficiency and cost savings long before they are trusted to manage customer-facing interactions.

The key question for your team this quarter isn't whether you should build your own shopping assistant. It's this: If an AI agent from Google, Microsoft, or a key customer tried to buy from you today, could it?
